Selected projects

Markets are made,
not merely observed.

Some of my projects begin outside a conventional field label: a luxury watch auction, an artist–manager contract, or a short-lived market for fractional claims on artworks. I use these settings to study how prices, rights, and liquidity are produced by institutions.

01 · Research infrastructure

Luxury Market Observatory

Price is produced.

The Luxury Market Observatory (LMO) studies how heterogeneous luxury assets acquire, transmit, and revise reference prices across market venues.

Rather than treating an observed transaction price as a primitive measure of value, the project follows the chain through which valuation is constructed: auctions generate public signals, those signals become reference points, resale markets transmit them, and subsequent transactions update the benchmark.

Core price chain

Auction → Reference → Resale → Update

Current settings

Watches · art auctions · secondary-market listings · alternative assets

02 · Theory · Artist–manager contracts

The Artist, the Manager, and the Right to Sell

Financing, Incentives, and Amendment Rights in Creative Contracts

When the manager sells the future, whose consent is required?

An artist signs with a manager because future career value depends on the manager’s continuing effort. What happens when the manager sells a claim on the compensation that was meant to sustain that effort?

The model starts from an artist–manager contract in the entertainment industry. The manager can monetize or transfer incentive-linked compensation without formally changing the artist’s contractual share, but the financing transaction changes the manager’s exposure to the artist’s future success and therefore the manager’s incentives. The central question is when the artist should hold consent or amendment rights over that sale.

Keywords

Artist–manager contracts Incomplete contracts Control rights Entertainment finance Incentive compatibility

Research question

When should an artist have the right to block or renegotiate a manager’s financing transaction?

03 · Market design · Alternative assets

Manufactured Liquidity

Fractional Ownership and Continuous Trading in an Opaque Real-Asset Market

Tradability is not the same as liquidity.

Can a trading platform transform opaque real assets into liquid securities simply by fractionalizing ownership and enabling continuous trading?

This project studies the Tianjin Cultural Artwork Exchange using the complete universe of 20 listed products. It traces product-level prices, volume, turnover, zero-trading days, platform rules, and regulatory interventions to separate initial tradability from durable liquidity and price discovery.

Empirical object

20 products · daily price and trading trajectories · market rules · regulatory archives

Core idea

Trading technology can manufacture initial tradability without creating durable market depth. The market can remain formally open while liquidity decays endogenously.

Why these settings?

Unusual markets reveal institutional primitives.

I am interested in settings where the object being traded is inseparable from the rules that make it tradeable.

Across these projects, the common object is institutional production: how a market creates a reference price, how a contract allocates the right to reshape incentives, and how a platform attempts to create liquidity from an otherwise illiquid asset.
