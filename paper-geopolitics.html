Geopolitical Value Expands the Extensive Margin of Official Export Credit

Evidence from Rotating Nonpermanent Membership on the United Nations Security Council

Under review at China Economic Quarterly (《经济学（季刊）》)JEL: F34 · F53 · H81

Research question

When a borrower country becomes temporarily more valuable geopolitically, does a policy lender offer cheaper loans to projects it would already finance—or approve additional projects at the access margin?

Abstract

Political value shifts which projects get funded

Geopolitical benefits can expand a policy lender’s project approval frontier without systematically changing observable loan terms.

Using rotating nonpermanent membership on the United Nations Security Council as a quasi-exogenous shock to geopolitical value, this paper studies Chinese official export credit from 2000 to 2023. In the first year of membership, projects without recorded commodity-revenue collateral increase significantly, while commodity-backed projects and observable loan terms do not change systematically.

The additional financing is concentrated in public infrastructure and industrial, mining, and construction projects. The evidence suggests that geopolitical returns affect policy lending mainly by expanding project access rather than changing loan prices or maturities.
