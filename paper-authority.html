When Formal Authority Moves Up

Rescaling in Third-Party Regulatory Markets

Tongpu Zhao & Li-An ZhouWorking paper

What happens to a market for regulatory intermediaries when formal approval authority moves upward, while implementation still depends on decentralized actors?

This project studies a large administrative rescaling of environmental-review approval in China. It asks how moving authority from counties to prefectures changes intermediary matching, screening, processing time, and the value of locally embedded experience.

Setting

Environmental impact assessment and approval

Data

Linked records on more than 960,000 projects, intermediary agencies, professionals, and clients

Selected presentation

University of Tokyo–Harvard-Yenching Institute Joint Training Program, 2026

Research design

Authority moves; markets adapt.

The empirical design links changes in the level of formal approval authority to shifts in who matches with whom, how long approvals take, and which forms of intermediary experience retain value after rescaling.

The broader question is whether state capacity is produced only inside the bureaucracy, or partly through the market of outside organizations that translate formal rules into implementation.
