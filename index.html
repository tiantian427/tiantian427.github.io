About

Research agenda

My research asks a recurring question: when formal authority moves, what changes on the ground?

I combine large-scale administrative data, regulatory records, judicial cases, market data, and text-based measurement to study institutional design in China. A recurring theme across my projects is the gap between formal reforms and the strategic responses they induce among governments, firms, intermediaries, and households.

Research

Selected working papers

I keep this page selective. The projects below best represent my current research agenda.

01

with Li-An Zhou · Working paper

Who Screens for the State? Recentralization and Local Control in Environmental Review

This paper studies a reform that shifted environmental review authority from county to city governments. Using large-scale project-level administrative data, we examine how recentralization changes local matching, screening, delay, and strategic adaptation rather than simply increasing enforcement capacity.

02

Reject and Resubmit · Management Science

Securing the Care Workforce: How Public Insurance Commitments Reshape Human Capital Pipelines

Using China’s Long-Term Care Insurance pilots and higher-education admissions data, this paper studies how public insurance commitments reshape nursing education, labor supply, and the broader care-service ecosystem.

03

Sole-authored working paper

The Shadow Fiscal Financier: Insurance Capital, Regulatory Shocks, and Local Government Debt in China

This paper studies insurance capital as a source of shadow fiscal financing. It uses liability-side regulatory shocks to identify how insurer demand affects local government financing vehicle credit spreads.
